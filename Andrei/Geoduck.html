Geoduck

Population Size

Not well documented

Life Span

Over 140 years

Burrow Depth

Up to 1 meter (3 feet)

Weight

0.5-1.5 kg (1-3 lbs)

Length

Up to 1.5 meters (5 feet) including the siphon

Description

The Geoduck (*Panopea generosa*) is a species of large, burrowing saltwater clam native to the coastal waters of the Pacific Northwest in North America. Known for its distinctive long siphon, the geoduck is one of the longest-living animals, with some individuals exceeding 140 years of age. Geoducks burrow deep into the sandy seabed and extend their siphons to filter plankton from the water. They are highly valued as a delicacy in various cuisines, particularly in East Asia. Despite their strange appearance, geoducks play an important role in maintaining healthy ecosystems by filtering water and supporting marine biodiversity.
